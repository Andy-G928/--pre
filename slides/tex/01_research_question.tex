\section{Research Question}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{Motivation: A Dual Role platform}
  Many large platforms now play two roles at the same time.

  (Eg. Amazon, JD.com, Apple App Store...)

  \begin{itemize}
    \item They run a marketplace for third-party sellers.
    \item They also sell their own products on the same marketplace.
  \end{itemize}

  \begin{block}{The key tension}
    The platform is both the market \textbf{organizer} and one of the \textbf{sellers} in the market.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{Why Dual Mode Is Controversial}
  Dual mode has both potential harms and potential efficiency benefits.

  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Regulatory concerns}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \textbf{Product imitation:} copy successful sellers using marketplace data.
          \item \textbf{Self-preferring:} steer consumers toward the platform's own products.
          \item \textbf{Innovation harm:} weaken third-party sellers' investment incentives.
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Potential benefits}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \textbf{One-stop shopping:} consumers can compare more options in one place.
          \item \textbf{Efficient supply:} the platform may be a better seller in some categories.
          \item \textbf{Price pressure:} the platform's product can constrain third-party prices.
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}

  \begin{block}{Policy question}
    Should regulators ban dual mode itself, or target specific harmful conduct?
  \end{block}
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{Research Question}
  The paper asks three linked questions.

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Does \textbf{banning} dual mode always \textbf{raise consumer surplus and welfare}?
    \item If dual mode is banned, does the platform \textbf{switch to seller mode or marketplace mode}?
    \item Are targeted \textbf{rules on imitation and self-preferring} better than a full ban?
  \end{enumerate}

  \begin{block}{Key Insight}
    The problem is not dual mode itself, but how the platform uses its power.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item A ban on dual mode is not always good for consumers or welfare.
        \item Rules that target imitation and self-preferring can work better.
    \end{itemize}
    
  \end{block}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{Location and Contribution}
  The paper is located at the intersection of platform economics and antitrust policy.

  \begin{itemize}
    \item It studies a platform that can choose among \textbf{marketplace mode}, \textbf{seller mode}, and \textbf{dual mode}.
    \item It connects the policy debate on platform bans with a formal welfare analysis.
    \item It separates \textbf{dual mode itself} from two possible bad conducts: \textbf{product imitation} and \textbf{self-preferring}.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
